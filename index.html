What's the difference between these types of assistance? ▸

All four are income/resource-based, but they're run by different agencies and cover different things — a client can fail one and clearly pass another, so they're worth screening separately rather than treating "assistance" as one yes/no question.

Extra Help (LIS)

Federal — Social Security Administration. Covers Part D only: the drug plan premium, deductible, and copays. Has nothing to do with Part A/B costs. Anyone who qualifies for an MSP or Medicaid below is automatically "deemed" eligible for Extra Help too, with no separate application.

Medicare Savings Program (QMB / SLMB / QI)

State-administered (through the state Medicaid agency), but not the same as full Medicaid. Pays Medicare's own Part A/B costs — QMB covers the premium plus deductibles and coinsurance; SLMB and QI cover the Part B premium only. Provides no medical benefits beyond that. In both Oklahoma and Texas, MSP's income limits are more generous than full state Medicaid ABD, so a client can qualify for MSP after failing the Medicaid check below.

State Medicaid (ABD)

State-administered, comprehensive health coverage on top of Medicare (doctor visits, hospital, and more) — not just cost-sharing help. Has the tightest income limit of the four. Real state Medicaid has more pathways to eligibility than this single income/resource line captures (e.g. a medically-needy spend-down) — a caseworker may still find a route for a client who fails this simplified screen.

SSI

Federal cash benefit from Social Security for aged, blind, or disabled people with very limited income and resources — not a Medicare program at all, but relevant because SSI recipients are typically automatically eligible for full Medicaid and Extra Help. Real SSI math has more exclusions (earned income, in-kind support) than this screen applies — SSA makes the actual determination.
